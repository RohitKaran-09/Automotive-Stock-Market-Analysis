This Week 5 capstone report presents an integrated investment strategy proposal for India's automotive stock market, combining the findings from the previous four internship tasks. It brings together market and segment trends, fundamental and technical analysis, comparative financial health, and the risk/opportunity assessment to form a structured, three-year research framework. The report is written for an investor audience and distinguishes evidence-based observations from assumptions and scenario judgements.

The market section reviews SIAM's Q2 FY2026–27 industry performance, Maruti Suzuki's latest Q2 update, including the tension between record volumes and lower EBIT amid commodity cost inflation, demand and product-mix trends, GST 2.0, PM E-DRIVE, the new CAFE framework, India's EV adoption trajectory, export opportunities, competitive investment and supply-chain/geopolitical risks. It integrates earlier M&M and Maruti research: FY26 financial comparisons, profitability and liquidity differences, cash-flow quality, business-mix considerations, and the fact that M&M consolidated figures include financial-services activity. The October 12 technical snapshot from the earlier analysis is treated strictly as historical evidence rather than a current buy/sell signal.

The proposal selects a diversified group of listed automotive companies across passenger and utility vehicles, two-wheelers, premium motorcycles, commercial vehicles, components, automotive technology and batteries. It supplies an illustrative model allocation of a 100% automotive-sector sleeve with a cash reserve, explains the role and key risk of every exposure, and sets single-stock and sector concentration limits. The sleeve is intended as a satellite within a broader diversified equity portfolio, not as a complete investment portfolio or a personalised recommendation.

The entry strategy uses a 40% / 30% / 30% staged deployment process, subject to fresh financial disclosures, conservative valuation ranges, a margin-of-safety test, operating confirmation and technical stabilisation. The return section presents three-year bear, base and bull sensitivity cases with explicit assumptions for earnings growth, terminal valuation change and dividend contribution. The scenarios are illustrative and are not probability-weighted forecasts, guaranteed returns or price targets.

Risk management includes a qualitative risk register, scenario stress tests, a drawdown review trigger, position caps, thesis-break rules, liquidity controls, monitoring indicators and quarterly/semi-annual rebalancing guidelines. The implementation roadmap assigns research, valuation, entry, evidence review and monitoring phases. Eight visual aids summarise demand, model allocation, risk/opportunity priorities, staged entry, scenario sensitivity, SWOT, the investor monitoring dashboard and the investment process. The package includes the final Word report, this submission description, source register, model portfolio, risk and opportunity registers, charts and a reproducible generation script. This is educational market research, not personalised investment advice or a buy/sell recommendation.
